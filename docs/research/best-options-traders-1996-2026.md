### 1.1 Jeff Yass / Susquehanna International Group

**(a) How they became the best.** No outside capital since May 1987 (FT 2024-10-20), so profits compounded without redemptions. Traders are manufactured in a three-month Bala Cynwyd class, not hired (Simkin, Knowledge Project #119). Adverse selection, not forecasting, is the first-order variable: "we are often on the bad end of informational asymmetry" (Simkin). Direction is hedged away at once so the P&L is the spread. Round 1 reported that the Tax Court found SIG "financially indifferent" to option direction and that a permanent, loss-making Firm Hedge has run since 1987 at a cumulative cost of $1.25–2.5bn (SIH Partners LLLP v. Commissioner, 167 T.C. No. 8, Aug 2026); **the docket could not be checked in either round, so these findings are unverified** and SIG's rank rests on the audited balance sheet and the ProPublica income series. Retail flow is purchased through affiliate GEB — $28.353m payable at 31 Dec 2023 (X-17A-5 FY2023).

**(b) Strategy mechanically.** Two-sided quotes on 50+ exchanges around a consensus fair value ("not something that comes out of a model. It's a consensus" — Tina Lindstrom, ex-SIG, The Derivative; the round-1 dossier misattributed the quote). Fill with theoretical edge, refuse or shade when the counterparty looks informed. Take the offsetting delta immediately; cover ~70% of the residual with related instruments and let ~30% ride (Abdelmessih, ReSolve Riffs). Vega is normalised by sqrt(time): "2,000 vega in a weekly option is more vol risk than your 10,000 vega in the 6-month" (Abdelmessih). Sizing is poker-derived; outcome is withheld in trade review to kill resulting bias.

**(c) Documented results.** Audited (SEC X-17A-5): member's equity $3,497m FY2017, $4,162m FY2018, $4,566m FY2019, $5,073m FY2020, $4,372m FY2021, $8,207m FY2022, $7,556m FY2023, $11,073m FY2025; total assets $109.4bn FY2025. This is balance-sheet scale, not P&L: the income-statement box is unchecked, and the FY2021/FY2023 declines are distributions. Major press: ProPublica IRS records give Yass's income $71m (1996), $66m (2005), −$470m (2008), >$1bn (2012), $1.26bn (2016), $2bn (2018). Forbes 2021: 1.8bn contracts in 2020, ~25% of US options trades (Alphacution). Refuted in round 1: "profits often topped $1bn in the late 1990s"; "largest or second-largest for 30 years"; "2.7bn contracts in 2023"; "only loss year in three decades".

**(d) Criticisms / failure modes.** 2008: "former Susquehanna traders believe the firm risked going out of business" (ProPublica). SEC cease-and-desist orders 2004 (SIG Specialists, $2.05m disgorgement + $988k penalty, 10b-5 scienter findings) and 2009 (Susquehanna Investment Group, $6.37m + $1.27m), ~42 SRO events on CRD 33875, and a June 2026 SDNY suit in which SIG says it lost >$70m as counterparty to alleged insider options trading in Futu/UP Fintech. Tax: $121m back taxes 2019; $170.8m QDI and $25.6m FTC disallowed Aug 2026 (same unverified docket). Yass's fortune is not purely options: the ByteDance stake (>$15bn on paper in 2020) was ~3x the market-making entity's equity (ProPublica). Per-trade edge compressed with the 0DTE mix shift (FT 2024).

**(e) Transfers / does not transfer.** Transfers: the adverse-selection filter (SPY/QQQ only, never event-driven), sizing as the dominant variable, outcome-blind logging, sqrt-time vega awareness (4–9 DTE carries more vol-of-vol per unit vega, not less), and — over a multi-year horizon — a permanent small tail budget. Does not transfer: the spread itself (Theta Gate pays it), purchased flow, continuous delta hedging, a proprietary surface, permanent capital.

### 1.2 Optiver Holding B.V.

**(a) How they became the best.** Founded 9 Apr 1986 as one EOE options seat; bought back all external stock by end-1998 (FinanceFeeds; the "cancelled IPO" is unsourced). Closed European floor trading in 2003 and went screen-only with in-house technology (US CBOE floor re-entered in 2013). Cross-product netting via a central risk book: "our pricing to counterparties improved without changing a single model" (Andrew Meyer, LinkedIn 2026). Latency is "the floor", models the differentiator (Pragmatic Engineer, Aug 2026). Governance: full Supervisory Board sits as Risk Committee, Automated Risk Committee, three lines of defence (Annual Reviews 2017, 2024). Equity kept at ~1.2x revenue (€5.49bn, 2025).

**(b) Strategy mechanically.** Continuous two-sided quotes across whole chains on index, single-stock, ETF, rate, FX and commodity options; fitted vol surface shifted for inventory, adverse selection, hedge liquidity and capital; residual risk externalised into correlated instruments. Caveat from the round-1 verifier: no primary Optiver source describes the pricing mechanics — only netting, custom hardware and the Amsterdam PMM quote-everything obligation are Optiver-specific and sourced.

**(c) Documented results.** Audited first-party series: NTI/net profit 2013–2017 €466m/€175m, €620m/€247m, €930m/€395m, €857m/€321m, €813m/€279m; 2019 €1,133m/€397m; 2020 €3.2bn/€1.4bn; 2021 €2.3bn/€958m; 2022 €3.29bn/€1,286m; 2023 €2.773bn/€1.158bn; 2024 €3.494bn/€1.369bn; 2025 €4.556bn/€1.769bn. Pre-2010 via KvK filings read by Amsterdamtrader (secondary): 2008 €228m, 2009 €6.3m. Bloomberg valued the firm at $17bn (Aug 2025). Every figure is consolidated multi-asset NTI; the options share is undisclosed.

**(d) Criticisms / failure modes.** Regime beta: −97% profit in 2009, −13% NTI 2019, −28% 2021, −16% 2023. Conduct: 2007 NYMEX "Hammer" banging-the-close (CFTC $13m + $1m, 2012; class action $16.75m, 2015); Consob €2.5m fine plus €2.7m frozen over Saipem naked shorting; a 2021 Amsterdam court finding Optiver "seriously culpable" toward a harassed female trader (>€400k); roughly a dozen exchange fines 2007–2026. The "20% Euro Stoxx 50 share" and "100+ exchanges" are marketing figures. **Withdrawn from round 1:** the claim that Optiver publishes guidance to avoid 0DTE and prefer 20–45 DTE. No Optiver publication exists; the 45-DTE-entry / 21-DTE-exit rule is tastylive's (Section 2, rank 15).

**(e) Transfers / does not transfer.** Transfers: price every trade as EV after costs, not premium collected; hard risk-appetite statements set in advance; pre-trade limits and a kill switch; limit orders at or inside mid on both legs; expect regime beta. Does not transfer: spread capture, speed, netting across thousands of instruments, exchange membership, €5.5bn of capital.

### 1.3 Thomas Peterffy / Timber Hill

**(a) How they became the best.** Own PDE pricer before Black-Scholes was published; "the odd duck constantly looking at his fair value sheets" on the AMEX in 1977 (Colossus 2025, SIFMA 2023). Spliced the Quotron wire (1982) and Nasdaq terminal line (1987); first handhelds on the floor (1983); first fully automated trader (1987). Traded guaranteed liquidity for exclusive electronic access (CBOE SPX 1987, DTB 1990, ISE 2000 at >50% of volume). Never unhedged after the 1977 DuPont loss (~$75–82k, "nearly half my trading capital"). >$1bn of excess regulatory capital in 2008 "allowed us to make markets continuously" (10-K FY2008).

**(b) Strategy mechanically.** ~577,000 products quoted; "calculate quotes a few seconds ahead of the market and execute small trades at a tiny but favorable differential"; every fill re-prices the whole portfolio "so that the next trade would be more likely to be a risk reducing than a risk increasing trade" (10-K FY2008). Profit driver: "our profitability is increased when actual volatility runs above implied" — the book was long realised-vs-implied, the mirror image of a premium seller.

**(c) Documented results.** Audited segment income before tax: 2006 $662.8m (69%), 2007 $719.8m (70%), 2008 $1,027.6m (76%), 2009 $330.8m, 2010 $89.5m ($88.2m restated), 2011 $413.2m, 2012 $188.6m, 2013 $71.9m, 2014 $114.1m, 2015 $130m, 2016 $44m, 2017 −$27m incl. ~$25m exit costs. Refuted in round 1: the 2010 −73% is partly a $243.9m currency-translation loss (non-GAAP −45%); the 430% ROE year was 1986; "1,300 underlyings" is Two Sigma's post-deal footprint; the $900m Goldman offer is Colossus/Steiner.

**(d) Criticisms / failure modes.** "I'm a computer programmer, and so are all of the most important people in my company" (II 2005) — the edge was technology and market structure, and it decayed to a loss within nine years of the peak: multiple listing (1999), penny pricing (2007), HFT pick-off of slow quotes (2010), and internalisers removing uninformed flow (SIFMA 2023). Automation errors: $3m phantom trades (1987), ~$2m from a missing interest-accrual term. Sanctions: CBOE 1989 false-statement fine, 2004 ISE QQQ position-limit breaches, an Austrian self-trade programming error. **Removed:** the "Swedish Supreme Court 2013" case; a Nordic algorithm-gaming matter (Norwegian, 2010–12) is cited only from a Brill chapter abstract and is not used.

**(e) Transfers / does not transfer.** Transfers: trade only off a fair-value view you trust; size so no single event takes half the account; many small uncorrelated positions; never trade into a catalyst you cannot price; limit orders; log every error and fix the process. Does not transfer: the P&L logic (long realised vol) and the volume that thin per-trade edges need. Hull Trading (Section 2, rank 11) is the direct comparator: same model-driven market making, exited at the top in 1999 rather than decaying to a loss.

### 1.4 Capstone Investment Advisors (Paul Britton)

**(a) How they became the best.** LIFFE pit trainee 1994; Amsterdam 1997–99 funded the Mako MBO; losing to Timber Hill's computers convinced him vol would become an asset class (FT 2018, via Wikipedia). Bought Mako's US arm in 2004; external capital from 2007. Q4 2008: short VIX from ~40 expecting a ceiling, VIX >80, covered at 65, AUM fell by "several hundred million", survived on a large European client (FT 2021 via Wikipedia). Since then a stated balanced long/short-vol posture: "short volatility strategies ... have limited ability to diversify when it matters most" (Capstone, Jan 2020). Solutions business (2010) sells convexity to pensions. The tail-mandate figures (+55% Aug 2015, +280% YTD by 16 Mar 2020, person-familiar) are **one-period payoffs, not returns**, and are not used as evidence of the flagship's quality.

**(b) Strategy mechanically.** Flagship (2007): multi-team book across equity vol RV (dispersion, term structure, skew), events/arb, FICC vol surfaces, risk-recycling of structured-product flow, trend, and a Volatility Carry sleeve (delta-hedged option selling for implied minus realised). Disclosed sizing: "target Risk Capital Ratio of approximately 160% of NAV", realised vol 6.2% (Mercer). 13F Q2 2026: 1,641 positions, top lines SPY puts, QQQ puts, NVDA puts, QQQ calls. Numeric Greek limits unpublished.

**(c) Documented results.** Independent (Mercer due-diligence sheet, El Camino Hospital packet, 9 Nov 2020), net calendar returns: 2008 −5.7, 2009 +10.4, 2010 +9.8, 2011 +12.5, 2012 +7.3, 2013 −0.4, 2014 −2.6, 2015 +4.0, 2016 +9.0, 2017 +7.5, 2018 +0.5, 2019 +10.3, 2020 Jan–Sep +5.0; annualised 5.2%, Sharpe 0.72, max DD −16.7%, vs HFRI RV Vol 3.0%. Press: ~7%/yr 2007–early 2021 and +20% in 2020 (FT 2021, person familiar). RAUM $91.5bn across 31 accounts (Form ADV, 5 Aug 2026) — a notional statistic. Comparison added in round 2: over an overlapping window the Cboe PUT index (gross, back-tested) did 7.1%/yr at Sharpe 0.51 with a −32.7% drawdown (2007–Jul 2026); Capstone's net series has a lower return and a materially shallower drawdown.

**(d) Criticisms / failure modes.** Returns are hedge-fund average for the risk. The Asia sleeve lost ~11% in March 2011 and closed. Regulatory record: Finnish FSA €20k (2016), CBOT $15k (2019). Hong Kong office opened ~2021–22, closed Sep 2025. Dispersion viewed as crowded via bank QIS. Most AUM growth is Solutions mandates ($9.9bn of $13.8bn). Capstone's own Oct 2025 paper asks whether a volatility risk premium still exists.

**(e) Transfers / does not transfer.** Transfers: never be structurally net short convexity; sell premium only when the VRP is measurably positive; "zero margin is the real risk" (Risk.net 2023); the 2008 lesson that a "VIX ceiling" thesis is a stop-loss failure. Does not transfer: variance swaps, exotics, dispersion, bank flow recycling, 6x notional, the fee franchise.

### 1.5 STRATEGY (R2): Cboe option-writing benchmarks and the volatility risk premium

This is a strategy family, not a person. It is ranked because PK's purpose is to choose a rule set for a defined-risk SPY/QQQ vertical book, and this is the only public, rule-based, 40-year series for that family. Verifier reliability: medium (every index figure reproduced; the Santa-Clara–Saretto block in the dossier overstated the paper).

**(a) How it became the best.** Rich index puts: implied volatility averaged 4.2 points above realised (VIX 19.3% vs realised 15.1%, 1990–2018, Bondarenko 2019; 4.1 points 1990–2022, Neuberger Berman); Goldman Sachs estimated ATM index options at 18.3% implied vs 16.0% realised over 1996–2007 (cited in Ennis Knupp 2008 — round-2 verifier corrected the attribution from Ennis Knupp to Goldman). Fat monthly carry: the ATM one-month SPX put collected 1.65%/month 1986–2008 (Ennis Knupp) and 1.85%/month 2006–2018 (Bondarenko). Full collateral and a mechanical roll meant no leverage and no margin calls. Convexity in bad months: in the 22% of months when the S&P 500 fell hard (avg −5.38%) PUT lost −2.93%; in the 49% of strong up-months (avg +4.14%) it made +2.11% (Ennis Knupp, 1986–2008). Put side beat call side: PUT outran BXM by 0.65%/yr (1986–92), 0.78% (1992–2004) and 2.11% (2004–14), part of it a settlement artefact (PUT is long the SOQ, BXM short the VWAP; Shalen 2015). Academic backbone: Bakshi–Kapadia (RFS 2003) — delta-hedged long S&P options lose on average, most at the money; Carr–Wu (RFS 2009) — strongly negative variance risk premia on S&P and Dow; Dew-Becker et al. (NBER 2015) — realised-variance exposure carries a Sharpe of about −1.7 for the buyer, the one-month claim −1.4, two months −0.5, three months or more about zero (verifier corrected the dossier's −1.7 for the one-month claim); Israelov–Nielsen (FAJ 2015) — the short-volatility sleeve of a covered call earned Sharpe 0.98 but was under 10% of the risk.

**(b) Strategy mechanically.** PUT: on each SPX monthly expiry sell one ATM SPX put, fully collateralised by T-bills, hold to expiry, roll same day. PUTY: same, 2% OTM. WPUT: one-week ATM puts rolled every Friday (gross premium 0.71%/week = 37.1%/yr vs PUT 1.85%/month = 22.1%/yr, 2006–2018). BXM: long S&P 500, sell one-month ATM call; BXMD sells a 30-delta call. CNDR: monthly iron condor, short ~20-delta put and call, long ~5-delta wings, T-bill collateral. BFLY: monthly ATM iron butterfly with 5% OTM wings. All are gross of costs and taxes and back-tested before launch (BXM 2002, PUT 2007, WPUT/BXMD/CNDR/BFLY 2015, PUTY 2019, PUTD 2022; history from 1986).

**(c) Documented results.** Wilshire (Mar 2019, for Cboe), Jun 1986–Dec 2018 monthly: PUT 9.54%/9.9% vol/Sharpe 0.64/MDD −35.5%/beta 0.47; BXM 8.50%/10.6%/0.51/−35.8%; BXMD 10.22%/12.8%/0.55/−42.7%; PPUT 6.64%/12.1%/0.29/−38.9%; S&P 500 9.80%/14.9%/0.45/−50.9%. Regimes (Wilshire Exh. 12): PUT beat the S&P 500 on return and Sharpe in 2000–06 (6.63% vs 2.16%) and 2007–09 (3.12% vs −3.72%), lagged in 1995–99 (20.44% vs 29.79%) and 2010–18 (7.09% vs 12.59%). Ennis Knupp (2008), Jul 1986–Oct 2008: PUT 10.32%/9.91%/0.63 vs S&P 8.77%/15.39%/0.30. Bondarenko (2019), Jan 2006–Dec 2018: PUT 5.97%/Sharpe 0.50/−32.7%, WPUT 4.51%/0.40/−24.2%, S&P 7.59%/0.51. Cboe fact sheets at 31 Jul 2026: PUT from 2007 7.1%/10.8%/0.51/−32.7%/beta 0.6 vs S&P TR 10.9%/15.5%/0.61; BXM from 1986 8.6%/Sharpe 0.55; BXMD 10.6%/0.61; PUTY 6.9%/8.7%/0.53/−28.9%; PUTD (dynamic, from 2006) 9.6%/0.65. Calendar PUT vs S&P TR: 2018 −5.9 vs −4.4; 2019 13.5 vs 31.5; 2020 2.1 vs 18.4; 2021 21.8 vs 28.7; 2022 −7.7 vs −18.1; 2023 14.3 vs 26.3; 2024 17.8 vs 25.0; 2025 9.2 vs 17.9. Dossier's own computation from Cboe CSVs, reproduced by the verifier to ±0.01 (month-end, 13-week bill rf): 2010–17 PUT 8.35%/Sharpe 0.95, WPUT 6.92%/0.98, CNDR −0.40%/−0.05, BFLY −3.93% vs S&P TR 13.92%/1.14; 2018–Aug 2026 PUT 8.05%/0.55, WPUT 2.66%/0.05, CNDR 0.91%/−0.23, BFLY −3.30%/−0.48 vs S&P TR 14.84%/0.77; 2008–09 CNDR +3.97%/−9.8% MDD while PUT −1.86%/−32.7% and S&P TR −10.74%/−48.5%. **Refuted by the round-2 verifier:** Santa-Clara–Saretto (JFM 2009) does not show a short strangle turning from +2.7%/month to −1.0%/month or Sharpes going negative; the accessible text gives the near 10% OTM put at 55%/month, SR 0.307, skew −10.7, worst month −22x premium, and margin calls cutting Sharpes by 35–75% (10% OTM put 0.304→0.095; 10% strangle 0.530→0.127 or 0.351 by version), with bid-ask spreads cutting short-put returns 4–6.9%/month. Also refuted: a 3.6%/yr T-bill figure attributed to Neuberger Berman (not in the paper; Ennis Knupp gives ~4.2%/yr for 1986–2008); PUT beta "0.47–0.56" understates the post-2006 beta (0.60 Cboe 2007–2026; 0.65 Bondarenko 2006–2018).

**(d) Criticisms / failure modes.** Back-tested and gross: Cboe's own disclaimer says many or most investors should expect to underperform the indexes. Sharpe flatters negative skew (PUT skew −2.10, kurtosis 9.7 vs S&P −0.81/2.5; worst month −17.65%, Oct 2008). Mostly equity beta: AQR's decomposition puts the short-vol sleeve at ~10% of BXM's risk and an uncompensated timing bet at ~25%. Regime dependence: PUT beat the S&P 500 only in low-return/high-vol regimes; since 2007 its Sharpe (0.51) is below the S&P 500 TR's (0.61) on Cboe's own sheet. Short tenor did not help: WPUT collected 37% gross premium a year against 22% for PUT yet returned less (2006–18) and has earned 2.7%/yr at Sharpe 0.05 since 2018. Defined-risk condors stopped paying: CNDR's long-run return is entirely pre-2010; 2010–17 −0.4%/yr and 2018–Aug 2026 +0.9%/yr at Sharpe −0.23, below T-bills for 16 years. Margin binds anyone not fully collateralised (Santa-Clara–Saretto: exchange haircuts of $7 margin per $1 ATM-put premium and $43 per $1 of 10% OTM premium; retail brokers 40%/35% vs exchange 15%/10%). Every performance study was commissioned or supported by Cboe.

**(e) Transfers / does not transfer.** Transfers: sell the put side, not the call side; keep the short strike near the money in the front tenor (Israelov–Tummala 2017: front-month, near-ATM to moderately OTM puts pay best per unit of stress loss; Dew-Becker: only ≤1–2-month variance claims are priced); never lever; judge the book against 50/50 SPY/T-bills, not against "income". Does not transfer: SPX cash-settled AM options, the SOQ artefact, gross accounting. The closest published analogues to a 4–9 DTE SPY/QQQ vertical — WPUT (weekly ATM puts) and CNDR (20/5-delta condor) — have earned roughly nothing since 2010–2018. At the credit/width ≈0.12 measured live in this project, a vertical's break-even win rate is ~88%, above the ~80% a 20-delta short strike implies, so the arithmetic edge is zero minus spread and commissions unless a real filter (vol regime, event avoidance) is added.

## 2. Ranks 6–18

## 5. Recommendation

Ranked options, one line each:

1. **The gated put-side vertical, benchmarked to PUT/PUTY** — Capstone/Dominicé's "sell only when the VRP is measurably positive" gate, tastylive's delta and 50% take-profit exits, sizing on width-at-expiry, judged monthly against Cboe PUTY and a 50/50 SPY/T-bill mix. Every component has either an independent series (Capstone, Mercer 2008–2020), a public 40-year index (Cboe), or three independent replications (tastytrade rule set).
2. **Warrington's operating rules** — sell 1–2 weeks out across several expiries, strikes 7–10% OTM on the put side in a mid-20s VIX, shrink before Fed/CPI and re-add after, cover the short leg rather than roll, and do not sell puts at all when VIX is beyond your entry zone (their March 2020 playbook). The longest survival record in the family, but the alpha is discretionary timing by a two-person team and is not a rule set you can code; the audited funds netted 3–5%/yr 2020–2025 and both sponsors dropped the strategy in 2025.
3. **SIG's epistemic layer** — sizing dominates being right, outcome-blind logging, adverse-selection filter. Cheapest to code; a discipline, not a strategy.

**Pick: option 1, implemented with option 2's VIX-zone and event rules as the concrete gate and option 3's logging as the review loop.** The Cboe series is the only evidence on the exact instrument family that is public and reproducible; the verifier reproduced every number. Warrington's rules are borrowed where they are mechanical (tenor, event sizing, cover-don't-roll, VIX zone) and not where they are discretionary (the band chart is unverified). Dominicé's closure and Warrington's decay set the ceiling: low single digits over T-bills for a disciplined retail-accessible short-premium index book, before Theta Gate's own spread and commission drag.

**Accepted violation: 4–9 DTE.** The six-session hackathon window forces 4–9 DTE. The evidence argues against it: tastylive's own rule is 45 DTE in / 21 DTE out; Dominicé ran 1–3 months; Abdelmessih's sqrt-time rule means 4–9 DTE carries more vol-of-vol per unit vega; Israelov–Tummala favour front-month but not sub-two-week tenor; and the weekly Cboe analogue underperformed the monthly one with 68% more gross premium (WPUT 4.51% vs PUT 5.97%, Sharpe 0.40 vs 0.50, 2006–2018; WPUT 2.66%/Sharpe 0.05 vs PUT 8.05%/0.55, 2018–Aug 2026). Expected cost of the violation: a Sharpe roughly 0.1–0.5 below the monthly variant from more rolls and more gap exposure, plus a proportionally larger bid-ask drag (Santa-Clara–Saretto: 4–6.9% of monthly return on near-term short puts in 1985–2001 data; Beckmeyer et al.: ~60% of retail 0DTE losses are transaction costs). This is a constraint, not a finding, and the log should record it as such.

**What edge is and is not being claimed over six sessions.** The only edge claimed is the put-side variance risk premium on SPY/QQQ, sold only when the gate says it is present. The evidence for that premium is real and long-dated: implied exceeded realised by 4.2 points on average 1990–2018 (Bondarenko), delta-hedged short S&P options earned positive returns in 1988–1995 (Bakshi–Kapadia) and 1996–2016 (AQR: a delta-hedged short 5% OTM put at 1.5%/yr, 2.2% vol, Sharpe 0.68), and the PUT index matched the S&P 500's return at two-thirds of its volatility from 1986 to 2018. The evidence that a retail-scale, short-tenor, defined-risk version captures it is weak: WPUT and CNDR have earned roughly nothing since 2018; the tastytrade rule set's independent replications sit at 0–5%/yr gross before costs and go negative at the allocations tastylive prescribes; Warrington's audited funds netted 3–5%/yr in 2020–2025 before both sponsors dropped them; Dominicé's UCITS netted ~2.9%/yr and closed; Capstone netted 5.2%/yr with a −16.7% drawdown and now asks whether the premium still exists. On a fairly priced chain credit/width is ~0.12, margin is the full width, and the arithmetic edge before the VRP is zero minus the bid-ask (PK's live test): the break-even win rate is ~88% against ~80% implied at a 20-delta short strike. Six trading sessions cannot distinguish that edge from noise: at 50% take-profit the winners capture roughly 0.06 of width each, and one loss at the 2x stop costs about four of them; one full-width loss costs about fifteen. The deliverable that is defensible in the window is the process — the gates, the width-defined sizing, the block-not-liquidate regime rule, the deterministic stops and the log — not the P&L.

Open question for PK: do you want the VRP and VIX-zone thresholds set from a short backtest on Alpaca bars before session one, or fixed by hand and logged as assumptions?
